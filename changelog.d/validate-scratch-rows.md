### Changed

- `validate`: the native HLOD delivery check loads its scratch rows in key order. Leaf points, segments and faces, ancestor requirements, coarse border images, coincident-face twins and multi-edge faces go through one bounded external sort at a time and are appended to the SQLite store, instead of being inserted at random keys. The cost per charged unit no longer grows with the archive: on the random-leaf class (`leafrand`) it stays between 6.0 and 7.5 ns on a P-core from 6 MB to 152 MB stored, where it rose from 7.8 to 26.7 ns at 61 MB. The payload-element time statement now holds for every measured class, including extrapolation to 1 GB stored: at most 0.18 s plus 1,492 s of CPU per stored GB on a P-core and 0.24 s plus 1,989 s on an E-core, at the additive budget. Verdicts are unchanged. Reports are unchanged too, except the running count in a budget refusal (`observed`) and the scratch peak, which now includes the sort runs. tex3 validation takes 25% less CPU, and Walem 42% less.
- `validate`: the HLOD delivery check charges the keyed lookups that coarse ancestors repeat at their measured cost. These are a coarse tile's descendant incidences, edge faces and coincident faces, and a coarse drop's border contacts: 320 units per seek and 32 per further row. Before, only the seek was charged (128 units), or one unit per row. Edge faces are indexed by direction, so such a lookup reads only rows it charges; before, every coarse ancestor read the other direction's rows uncharged. Small archives dominated by these lookups charge up to about 54% more units. All such archives measured stay far under the 16-million-unit floor, so only an explicit `totalPayloadElements` budget can change their verdict. A coarse drop's border-contact check now seeks each side of the tile's range. The D4 coarse-identity check reads each reference once instead of once per ancestor reference.
- `validate`: legitimate mesh output with many tiny primitives validates at the default budget. Before, it could be refused on `totalPayloadElements`. The coarse-drop recount tries first the coarse face that answered its previous distance query, which gives the same answers with far less work: 46 million units became 7 million on the PR #306 review fixture, and its validation takes 0.25 s instead of 0.65 s. The open-segment census is charged 32 units per open edge, its measured cost, instead of 256. With the additive budget above, every dense tiny-triangle output measured (34 bakes from 184 KB to 7.5 MB: isolated, repeated, jittered, quad and double-sided layouts) validates with at least a 1.5× margin.

### Changed

- `validate`: the native HLOD delivery check loads its scratch rows in key order. Leaf points, segments and faces, ancestor requirements, coarse border images, coincident-face twins and multi-edge faces go through one bounded external sort at a time and are appended to the SQLite store, instead of being inserted at random keys. The cost per charged unit no longer grows with the archive: on the random-leaf class (`leafrand`) it stays between 7.3 and 8.8 ns on a P-core from 1.5 MB to 152 MB stored, where it rose from 7.8 to 26.7 ns at 61 MB. The payload-element time statement now holds for every measured class, including extrapolation to 1 GB stored: at most 855 s of CPU per stored GB on a P-core and 1,087 on an E-core. Verdicts are unchanged. Reports are unchanged too, except the running count in a budget refusal (`observed`) and the scratch peak, which now includes the sort runs. tex3 validation takes 25% less CPU, and Walem 42% less.
- `validate`: the HLOD delivery check charges the keyed lookups that coarse ancestors repeat at their measured cost. These are a coarse tile's descendant incidences, edge faces and coincident faces, and a coarse drop's border contacts: 320 units per seek and 32 per further row. Before, only the seek was charged (128 units), or one unit per row. Edge faces are indexed by direction, so such a lookup reads only rows it charges; before, every coarse ancestor read the other direction's rows uncharged. Small archives dominated by these lookups charge up to about 54% more units. All such archives measured stay far under the 16-million-unit floor, so only an explicit `totalPayloadElements` budget can change their verdict. A coarse drop's border-contact check now seeks each side of the tile's range. The D4 coarse-identity check reads each reference once instead of once per ancestor reference.
